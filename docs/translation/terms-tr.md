# Turkish (`tr`) binding terminology — Talmud Eser Sefirot

Every Turkish translator reads this before the first item. It is built only from
Bnei Baruch's **official Turkish** and counted, not remembered:

| code  | source                   | what it is                                                                              |
| ----- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **I** | `km-intro/intro.tr.txt`  | _On Sefirot’un Çalışılmasına Giriş_ (155 sections) — the Introduction                   |
| **P** | `km-intro/pticha.tr.txt` | _Kabala İlmine Giriş_ (185 of 187 sections) — the Petiha, the technical-vocabulary text |
| **C** | —                        | **none.** The corpus has no Turkish BB TES (`*.tr-bb.json` does not exist).             |

Counts are Python-regex matches over I and P, written `total (I.. P..)`; "Hebrew N"
is the same count over `intro.he.txt` + `pticha.he.txt`, for alignment. Before
counting, the space BB's HTML leaves between an italic term and its suffix
(`Masah ’ın`, 4,617 times) is removed — see §1.6. 280 rows: **247 OFFICIAL**,
**33 PROPOSED**. The counter is `terms/tr/work/cnt.py`, the table is generated by
`terms/tr/work/gen.py` from `rows_g1.tsv` + `rows_extra.tsv`; re-run any number
with them (§6).

**The Petiha is the authority for technical terms.** Its Turkish and Hebrew line
up almost one to one: מסך 243 ↔ Masah 257, עביות 180 ↔ Aviut 181, רשימו 67 ↔
Reşimo+Reşimot 67, זווג 100 ↔ Zivug+Zivugim 100, ביטוש 30 ↔ Bituş 31, כלים 185 ↔
Kelim 183, מוחין 97 ↔ Mohin 96, או"ח 48 ↔ Or Hozer 47, או"מ 49 ↔ Or Makif 49,
או"א 152 ↔ AVİ 150. That is the main finding: **BB's Turkish transliterates
almost the whole technical vocabulary** — far more than BB's Spanish, French or
Russian do — and glosses each term once in parentheses.

## 0. Precedence

1. **This table binds.** Where it is silent, grep P (technical) or I (ethical,
   religious) for BB's form, then follow the scheme in §1. Report every new term
   you had to coin.
2. **BB's Turkish beats the English canon.** `docs/translation-terminology.md` is
   orientation only. The big differences (§3.1): screen, coarseness, record,
   coupling, clothing, clash, refinement, phase, reflected/surrounding/inner/direct
   light, restriction, expansion, shells, posterior are all **transliterated** in
   Turkish (Masah, Aviut, Reşimo, Zivug, Hitlabşut, Bituş, Hizdakhut, Behina,
   Or Hozer/Makif/Pinimi/Yaşar, Tsimtsum, Hitpaştut, Klipot, Ahoraim), and
   phase four is **Behina Dalet**, not "dördüncü safha".
3. **Religious vocabulary uses BB's universal register** (§1.4): Torah and
   Mitzvot are _Maneviyat ve Islahlar_, a rabbi is _Kabalist_, God is _Yaradan_,
   Israel is _halk_, the Shechina is _Kutsallık_. This is deliberate in BB's
   Turkish and it binds.
4. **The lemma rule, as in English.** A bolded `<b>` lemma quoting the Ari is just
   bold, exactly as in the pane: keep the `<b>…</b>` tags, translate the quoted
   words, follow the pane's _phrasing_. **Technical terms and name spellings in
   this table never follow the pane** — inside a lemma still write Masah, Behina
   Dalet, Hohma, AVİ.
5. **Never emend, never drop** — unchanged from `docs/translation-rules.md`. BB's
   own Turkish _drops_ citation formulae (עכ"ל, עש"ה, ד"ה); our translation does
   not — it renders them with the PROPOSED wordings of §2.9.

**Hebrew plurals are kept**, never Turkish -lar: Sefirot, Partzufim, Kelim,
Behinot, Reşimot, Zivugim, Gufim, Roşim, Klipot, Nekudot, Otiot, Taamim, Tagin,

Turkish plural suffix on a Hebrew plural (BB's AHP’ları 9, Behina’lar 2) is BB's
own rare looseness — avoid it.

**Turkish capital İ.** Capital _i_ is dotted: **AVİ** (150 : AVI 0), **İma** 14,
**İbur** 14; in BB's all-caps headings PARTZUFİM, HİTLABŞUT. Lower-case dotless ı
appears only in Turkish words (ıslah, ışık).

### 1.2 Capitalisation

- **Işık** is always capitalised for אור: 535 against 17 lower-case (daylight,
  and BB's five "Hohma ışığı", which you should write **Or Hohma**). Plural
  **Işıklar** 84 : 0. Named lights: **Üst Işık** 79 : 0, Eyn Sof Işığı, Yaşam
  Işığı, Haya Işığı.
- **Every transliterated term is capitalised**: Sefirot (280 : sefirot 0), Sefira,
  Partzuf, Behina, Masah, Aviut, Reşimo, Zivug, Kli, Kelim, Roş, Guf, Klipot, the
  Sefirot, Partzufim, worlds and lights.
- **Translated technical nouns are lower-case**: dünya, seviye, derece, kırılma,
  ıslah, çizgi, kök, boşluk, alma arzusu, ihsan etme arzusu, form eşitliği, alma
  kabı, erkek, dişi.
- **dünya is lower-case after a name**: Atzilut dünyası (166 : Dünyası 0); the
  numeral of "on Sefirot" is lower-case (208 : 8, the 8 being the book title and
  sentence starts).
- **The spiritual "upper" and "above" are capitalised** in BB: **Üst Derece** 61 :
  Üst derece 9; **Yukarıdan aşağıya** 29 : 0; **aşağıdan Yukarıya** 9 : 0; Üst
  Dünyalar. **alt derece** (37) stays lower-case, and so does textual
  _yukarıda bahsedilen_ (כנ"ל).
- **God and His pronouns** are capitalised: Yaradan, Yaratıcı, **O, O’nun** (142 :
  o’nun 0), Kendisi, O’nun Özü.
- **Reference labels** before a number: **Madde** N (capital), **Bölüm** N,
  **Kapı** N, but lower-case **sayfa** N, as BB writes them (§2.9).

### 1.3 Acronyms

BB writes every Hebrew acronym **in capitals without vowels** (unlike BB's
Spanish GaR/ZoN): ZA 70, ZON 125, AVİ 150, YEŞSUT 34, AHP 88, AA 58, AK 340,
ABYA 39, BYA 72, GAR 59, VAK 49, ZAT 68, MAN 57, KHB 26, HGT 23, NHY 19, NHYM 14,
HB 17, GE 20, TNHY 6, NRNHY 3, NRN 20, AB 98, SAG 128, MA 106, BON 48. The one
mixed-case form is the Name **HaVaYaH** (8). Pairs are space-separated: **HB TM**,
**KHB TM**, **KHB ZON**, **AB SAG**, **HGT NHY**.

Suffixes follow the acronym **as it is spoken** (§1.6): AK’ın (271 : AK’nın 22),
AB’ın 45, SAG’ın 92, AHP’ın 58, ZA’nın 13, MA’nın 29, ZON’un 67, BON’un 32,
AVİ’nin 63, GAR’ı 40, VAK’ı 15.

Spell out an acronym only where the Hebrew spells it out: Zeir Anpin, Arih Anpin
(BB 1), Adam Kadmon, Aba ve İma, Yisrael Saba ve Tevuna (PROPOSED forms).
TANTA is expanded: Taamim, Nekudot, Tagin ve Otiot.

**Trap — Islah vs ıslah.** Capital **Islah / Islahlar** = מצוה/מצוות (the
commandments); lower-case **ıslah** = תיקון (correction). Turkish capitalises a
sentence's first word, so never start a sentence with ıslah = תיקון: reorder
("Bu ıslah …", "Tikun dünyasında …").

TES is overwhelmingly technical, so this register touches few items — but where
the Ari or Baal HaSulam say תורה ומצוות, צדיקים, השכינה or הקב"ה, write the
forms above.

### 1.5 Quotation marks

- Outer quotes: **“…”** (511 open / 503 close). Never «…» (0) or straight "…" (0).
- Inner quotes: **‘…’** (29).
- The apostrophe before a suffix is the typographic **’** (U+2019) — 614 in BB
  (after removing the italic artefact), straight ' 0. It is the same character as
  the inner closing quote; that is normal Turkish typography.

### 1.6 Suffixes on transliterated terms

Turkish case and possessive suffixes attach to every Hebrew term with ’:
Masah’ın 124, Kelim’in 118, Roş’un 143, Sefirot’un 185, Partzuf’un 106, Kli’si 62,
Malhut’un 72, Behina’sı 24, Atzilut’un 154.

- **No space before ’.** The raw BB text shows `Masah ’ın` 4,617 times: the term
  was italic in BB's HTML and the suffix was not. It is an export artefact, not
  a convention. Write `Masah’ın`.
- **Vowel harmony follows the pronunciation**: AK’ın, AB’ın, Pe’i (57; BB treats
  Pe as Peh), Kli’nin, Hey’in, Daat’ın.
- **BB writes -da/-de/-dan/-den after the apostrophe even after a voiceless
  consonant** — Partzuf’da 23 (Partzuf’ta 0), Dalet’den 31 (Dalet’ten 0),
  Masah’dan 5 (Masah’tan 0); overall 265 : 6. Standard Turkish orthography would
  write -ta/-tan; BB does not, and its usage binds. Same with numerals: Madde
  15’deki, Madde 24’de (11 : 0).

### 1.9 Markup and numbers

- Keep the pane's `<b>…</b>` around the Ari's quoted lemma; no extra quotes.
- Hebrew-letter numbers become Arabic numerals by gematria: (ב) → (2), אות י"א →
  Madde 11, דף רצ"ז → sayfa 297.
- Phase ordinals are **Hebrew letter names**: Behina Alef / Bet / Gimel / Dalet
  (Dalet 126 — never "Behina 4", never "dördüncü safha").

### 3.2 Sense splits and false friends

- **Or Pinimi ≠ İç Işık.** _Or Pinimi_ is the technical inner light (25). _İç Işık_
  is the name of Baal HaSulam's first commentary layer (UI label; BB attests the
  words only as the gloss of Or Pinimi). `או"פ` can be either — read the sentence:
  "İç Işık’ta (Madde 5)" is a citation, "Or Pinimi ve Or Makif" is the concept.
- **izlenim is three things in BB — do not let it stand for any term.** BB uses
  _izlenim_ for הבחנה ("dört izlenim" = ד' הבחנות, 84 in all), as the gloss of
  Reşimot ("(izlenimler)"), and as one gloss of Behina. In body text write
  **Behina** for בחינה, **Reşimo** for רשימו, and keep _izlenim_ for הבחנה only.
- **seviye ≠ derece.** seviye = קומה (359); derece = מדרגה (281). Both appear in
  one sentence; never swap them.
- **Islah ≠ ıslah** (§1.4): capital = the commandments, lower-case = תיקון.
- **Kutsallık is two Hebrew words.** BB uses it for קדושה _and_ for שכינה. Where
  both occur in one item, keep the Hebrew sense clear by context ("Kutsallığın
  ilhamı" = השראת השכינה, I§33).
- **Yaradan ≠ Yaratıcı.** Yaradan = בורא / הקב"ה (177); Yaratıcı = מאציל (BB
  P§7, §13) and, in I, "his Maker" (Yaratıcısına). Keep this split so that
  "the Emanator" and "the Creator" stay distinguishable.
- **erdemli ≠ Tsadikim.** Moral צדיק → erdemli (I); the technical נר"ן דצדיקים that
  raise MAN → Tsadikim’in NRN’si (P§160–168).
- **Merhamet is two glosses.** BB glosses both Or Hasadim (Merhamet Işığı) and
  Rahamim (merhamet). In body text keep the Hebrew terms; the Turkish word is a
  gloss only.
- **Hohma ≠ ilim.** The Sefira is always Hohma. חכמת הקבלה → Kabala ilmi (23);
  חכמה in a gloss → Or Hohma (Bilgelik Işığı).
- **Kli ≠ kap.** Kli/Kelim for כלי/כלים; Turkish _kap_ only in BB's fixed _alma
  kabı / alma kapları_ (22) and in parables.
- **Nekudot vs nokta; Otiot vs harfler; Taamim vs neden.** Nekudot/Otiot/Taamim
  are the TANTA terms; a single point = nokta (35); letters of a word = harfler;
  טעם meaning "reason" = neden / sebep, never Taamim.
- **Raglaim vs Raglin.** Write the form the Hebrew has: Sium Raglin (17),
  Etzbaot Raglin (2) for the Aramaic compounds; Raglaim (5) for bare רגלים.
- **Pe and Hey are also letters.** Pe (ağız) is the part of a Partzuf; "Pe harfi"
  is the letter פ. Hey (37) is always the letter.
- **Gimatria (numeroloji) is a false friend.** BB's gloss "numeroloji" suggests
  fortune-telling in Turkish; gloss Gimatria (sayısal değer) instead.
- **Âdem.** אדם (the created man) → Âdem (7, with â); אדם הראשון → Adam ha Rişon
  (2). Ordinary "a person" = kişi / insan.
- **Two-sense abbreviations — read the clause:**
  - `ע"ש`: oraya bakın, or `על שם` → adıyla anılır.
  - `ה"ר`: ilk Hey, or `ה' ראשונות` → ilk beş.
  - `ה"ס`: … sırrıdır, or `ה' ספירות` → beş Sefirot.
  - `ז"א`: ZA, or `זאת אומרת` → yani.
  - `א"א`: AA, or `אי אפשר` → imkânsızdır.
  - `אות`: Madde N, or harf (`אות ה'` = Hey harfi).
  - `תשובה`: Cevap N, or tövbe.

## 4. Mismatches with the repo (flagged, not changed — the repo is read-only for this task)

1. **Book title.** `i18n/locales/tr.json` calls TES **"On Sefirot'un Çalışması"**
   (heroSubtitle, description, intro). BB names the book **"Talmud Eser Sefirot
   (On Sefirot’un İncelenmesi)"** (I§156) and "On Sefirot’un İncelenmesi" (P§1); BB's
   _Introduction_ is titled "On Sefirot’un **Çalışılmasına** Giriş". The UI is
   therefore half-supported. This table rules BB's book name for translated text;
   whether the UI should change is the owner's call.
2. **Apostrophe.** The UI writes straight `'` (Ari'nin, Sefirot'un, Baal
   HaSulam'ın); BB writes `’` (614 : 0). The translations will use `’`, so the
   site will mix both until the UI is updated.
3. **"Ohr Pnimi" in the UI** (`innerLightAbsent`: "İç Işık (Ohr Pnimi)"). BB's
   spelling is **Or Pinimi** (25 : Ohr/Or Pnimi 0).
4. **"Rav Yehuda Aşlag"** (`portraitAlt`). BB's two texts never use Rav (0);
   their word for a rabbi is Kabalist. Baal HaSulam's own name is not in BB's
   texts, so this is flagged, not ruled.
5. **No Turkish BB TES exists in the corpus**, so nothing here is checked against
   BB's own Turkish of TES itself — only against the Introduction and the Petiha.
   The Introduction's Turkish is also heavily **abridged** (§156 drops the names of
   both commentary layers and the Table of Questions/Answers details), which is why
   the section names of §2.7 are PROPOSED from the UI.
6. The brief expects 187 Petiha sections; the Turkish file has 185 detectable
   section numbers (two are merged, e.g. "98-99."). Counts are over the whole
   text, so nothing is lost.

## 5. Decisions I am least sure of

1. **-da/-dan after voiceless consonants (§1.6).** Binding BB's 265 : 6 means
   writing Partzuf’da, Masah’dan, Madde 24’de against standard Turkish orthography
   (TDK: -ta/-tan). It is the official usage, but an editor may prefer TDK.
2. **מאציל → Yaratıcı.** Rests on two aligned passages (P§7, §13) plus one
   "Kaynak (Yaratıcı)". TES uses מאציל constantly; if the owner wants a distinct
   word, "Kaynak" is BB's only alternative.
3. **Igulim / Yosher translated** (daireler / doğrusallık, PROPOSED) while BB's
   Turkish transliterates nearly everything else. The only anchor is that BB
   translates קו as çizgi (12). Transliterating them (İgulim, Yoşer) is equally
   defensible.
4. **Citation wordings BB never writes**: "“…” diye başlayan pasaj" (ד"ה),
   "orada iyice inceleyin" (עש"ה), "alıntının sonu" (עכ"ל), "Cevap N", "Kısım N".
   They recur thousands of times, so a wording change later is expensive.
5. **Book title** — İncelenmesi (BB, 2) over Çalışması (UI).
6. **Hitlabşut / Hitpaştut as nouns, Turkish verbs otherwise.** BB's own split is
   not clean (kıyafetlenme as a noun 29 times). Translators will have to judge
   noun vs verb in every sentence.
7. **ישראל → halk** and the ban on İsrail. BB's evidence is the Introduction (10)
   plus "İnsan" in P§1, §10. TES rarely says ישראל, but where it names the
   Partzuf the PROPOSED "Yisrael Saba ve Tevuna" is untested.
8. **Rabbi Shimon Bar Yochai → Kabalist Şimon Bar Yohay** and **רשב"י → Raşbi**:
   built from BB's Kabalist Şimon (13) and Raşi (5); BB never names him.
9. **Kutsallık for both קדושה and שכינה.** Faithful to BB, but it erases a
   distinction TES sometimes relies on.
10. **Gloss choices that override BB's own**: Gimatria (sayısal değer) instead of
    BB's "(numeroloji)"; Mohin kept with BB's "(Işık)" although it collides with
    Işık = אור; Gadlut (büyüklük) chosen from BB's three-way gloss.

## 6. How to re-count

```
cd terms/tr/work
python3 -I gen.py                      # rebuilds terms-tr.md + terms-tr.json, validates
printf 'Masah\t\\bMasah\\b\n' | python3 -I cnt.py   # count any form: label<TAB>regex
python3 -I cnt.py ctx 'Tsadikim' 5     # show contexts
python3 -I sx2.py pticha 'מסך' 'Masah' 3   # Hebrew section ↔ Turkish section
```

`gen.py` refuses to build if an OFFICIAL row counts 0, a PROPOSED row has an
official count, a banned pattern occurs in BB's texts or matches a binding form,
or a Hebrew trigger fires on its known false positives (ודו"ק, תנה"י, כחב"ד, אבי"ע,
הרבה, אותיות, תשובה מיראה).
